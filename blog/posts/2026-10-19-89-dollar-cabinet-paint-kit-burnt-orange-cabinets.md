> An $89 kit covers oak cabinets in the burnt-orange tone HGTV and BHG are pushing this week. What reviewers say it actually takes to pull off.

Every contractor quote for dated oak cabinets starts the same way: tear them out, order new boxes, budget $5,000 to $20,000 and three to six weeks of a kitchen you can't cook in. I looked at what that actually costs to execute at home, starting with an $89 cabinet paint kit, and what the people who've already used it on oak say about getting the color and the finish right.

## The Replacement-Cost Math Nobody Mentions First

Industry cost guides consistently put full cabinet replacement for an average kitchen at $5,000–$20,000, and professional refacing — keeping the boxes, swapping doors and veneer — at roughly $4,000–$10,000. An $89 cabinet paint kit isn't a like-for-like substitute for either; it won't fix water damage or warped doors. But for cabinets that are structurally sound and just dated or glossy, it's the same transformation at roughly 1–2% of the replacement cost.

The Rust-Oleum Cabinet Transformations kit — the product driving most of the 'paint your cabinets' searches right now — ships in fixed color/finish combinations (Dark, Linen, Espresso, White, and a few others), not a pre-mixed burnt-orange. That matters because the burnt-orange and terracotta cabinets showing up on Pinterest and in the HGTV/BHG coverage this week aren't coming out of the box that color. DIYers report taking the kit's bonding coat to a paint counter and having it custom-tinted to match a specific chip — which is also why results vary more than with a pre-mixed paint: tint ratio, oak's natural grain, and how many coats go on all shift the final tone.

Before any of that tinting happens, the cabinet surfaces have to be stripped of grease and cooking residue, since the bonding coat is formulated to grip a clean, deglossed surface — not a film of kitchen grime.

## What's Actually in the $89 Box — and Where the Process Gets Messy

A Cabinet Transformations-style kit typically bundles a liquid deglosser/cleaner, a bonding coat (the step that lets new paint grip old finished wood without full sanding), scuff pads, and a protective top coat — some kits add a glaze step for depth. Rust-Oleum's own labeling lists coverage around 200 square feet per kit, which translates to roughly 20–25 standard doors and drawers. A kitchen with a double run of upper and lower cabinets commonly needs two kits, not one, which changes the real cost from $89 to closer to $160–$180.

Reviewers who've worked through the full process consistently flag three things: the deglosser has a strong solvent smell that needs real ventilation, not just a cracked window; the bond coat goes on thin and needs two to three light coats rather than one heavy one, or it stays tacky; and label-stated cure times run longer than people expect — dry-to-touch in about 2 hours, but light use closer to 3 days and full cure stretching toward 1–3 weeks depending on the specific product line. Doors come off the hinges for most of that window, which is also the only practical time to deal with whatever's been shoved in the back of the cabinets for years.

Bagging up solvent-soaked rags, sanding dust, and painter's tape as you go matters more here than in a typical paint job, since oil-based deglossers and bond coats aren't something you want sitting loose in a kitchen trash can for a week.

## FAQ

**Does the Rust-Oleum Cabinet Transformations kit come in burnt-orange?**

Not as a pre-mixed option. The kit ships in fixed color/finish sets like Dark, Linen, Espresso, and White. The burnt-orange and terracotta cabinets showing up this week come from DIYers having the included bonding coat custom-tinted at a paint counter to match a specific chip, which is also why results vary between projects.

**How many cabinets will one $89 kit actually cover?**

Rust-Oleum's own labeling lists coverage around 200 square feet, roughly 20–25 standard doors and drawers. A kitchen with a full run of upper and lower cabinets commonly needs two kits, putting the real cost closer to $160–$180.

**Do I need to sand oak cabinets before using the kit?**

The kit's bonding coat is designed to skip full sanding, but reviewers consistently note that scuff-sanding any glossy or heavily varnished spots still improves adhesion and reduces peeling risk compared to skipping it entirely.

**How long until the cabinets can be used normally again?**

Per the product label, expect dry-to-touch around 2 hours, safe for light use around 3 days, and a full cure stretching 1 to 3 weeks depending on the specific kit. Rehanging doors too early is the most commonly reported cause of tackiness and marking.

**Is painting cabinets actually cheaper than refacing or replacing them?**

Yes, by a wide margin for structurally sound cabinets. Full replacement commonly runs $5,000–$20,000 and professional refacing $4,000–$10,000, versus $89–$180 in kit cost for a full painted kitchen, plus hardware.

**Will the paint hide oak's visible wood grain?**

Not fully. The bonding coat is formulated to stick to oak's existing finish and grain, but the natural texture of oak grain often still shows faintly through the paint unless the surface is sanded smoother or filled first.
