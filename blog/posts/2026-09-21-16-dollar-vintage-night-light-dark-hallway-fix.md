## FAQ

**Do antique-look plug-in night lights come with a bulb included?**

Most generic listings in this category ship with an LED pre-installed, typically rated equivalent to a 15–25 watt incandescent bulb. A smaller number use a candelabra-base bulb sold separately, so check the specific listing's bulb-included field before ordering.

**Should I get a warm white or cool white plug-in light for a cozy hallway?**

For the "cozy fall" aesthetic, look for a 2700K–3000K warm-white rating. Cool white (5000K and up), like the motion-sensor light covered here, is better suited to functional safety lighting near floors and bathrooms rather than ambiance.

**Will running a plug-in night light all night raise my electric bill?**

LED night lights typically draw under 1 watt. Run continuously, that costs roughly $1 or less per year at average U.S. electricity rates, so cost isn't a meaningful factor in choosing between these products.

**Can renters use any of these products without drilling into walls?**

Yes. Every product in this roundup either plugs directly into an outlet, mounts with adhesive, or hangs on a tension rod — none require wall anchors or permanent hardware.

**How much light does a $16 vintage sconce night light actually put out?**

Generic listings in this category commonly list output equivalent to a 15–25 watt incandescent bulb — enough to remove total darkness and create ambiance, not enough to read by.

**What's the cheapest way to layer this look if I only have $15 to spend?**

The Minetom Fairy Lights at $11.99 or the LED Motion Sensor 2-pack at $11.99 both fit under $15 individually and cover the two most-requested parts of this trend: ambient warmth or hallway safety.
