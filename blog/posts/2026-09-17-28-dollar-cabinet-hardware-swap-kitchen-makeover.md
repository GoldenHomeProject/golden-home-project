## Measure Before You Order: Hole Spacing, Length, and Screw Size

The single most common regret in cabinet-pull reviews across every brand, not just Ravinte's, is buying a length that doesn't match existing holes. Here's the actual measurement process:

- **Center-to-center spacing**: measure from the middle of one existing screw hole to the middle of the other, in inches. Common spacings are 3 inches, 3.75 inches, 4 inches, and 5 inches. This number is non-negotiable — a pull with the wrong spacing simply will not mount without new holes.
- **Overall pull length vs. hole spacing**: these are different numbers. A 5-inch pull typically has 3.75-inch hole spacing, with the extra length as overhang on each end. Manufacturers list both; check the listing's dimension diagram, not just the name.
- **Screw length**: cabinet doors run roughly 0.75 inches thick, drawers can run up to 1 inch. Pulls generally ship with screws sized for standard cabinet-door thickness; thicker drawer fronts sometimes need the longer screws separately, which is the second-most-common complaint in reviews after spacing mismatches.
- **Existing knob-to-pull conversion**: if you're going from a single-hole knob to a two-hole pull, you'll be drilling a new hole regardless of spacing math — plan for wood filler on the old hole if it won't be covered by the new pull's backplate.

If you're replacing knobs with pulls of a different spacing, or moving from knobs to pulls entirely, budget an extra 20 minutes per door for filling and touch-up paint on the old holes, plus a drill. If your existing spacing matches, skip straight to a screwdriver and this becomes a same-day project with no tools beyond what's in a kitchen junk drawer already.

## FAQ

**Will these matte black pulls fit my existing cabinet holes?**

Only if your existing hole spacing is 3.75 inches center-to-center, which is the spacing on the Ravinte 5-inch pulls. Measure center-to-center on one existing pull or knob before ordering — 3-inch, 4-inch, and 5-inch spacings are also common and won't match without drilling new holes.

**Do I need to paint or refinish the cabinets themselves for this to look right?**

No. This swap works on the assumption the cabinet boxes and doors are in acceptable condition and only the hardware finish is dated. If the cabinet wood or paint itself is damaged or peeling, hardware alone won't fix that — the fix at that point is paint or refacing, a different budget tier entirely.

**How many pulls does an average kitchen actually need?**

Most kitchens with 10-14 cabinet doors and 6-8 drawers need 16-22 pulls total. The 30-pack leaves 8-14 spares, which covers a pantry cabinet, a bathroom vanity, or simple mounting mistakes without a second order.

**What's the real cost difference between this and hiring a contractor to do it?**

A DIY hardware installation with a $26.99 multi-pack and a screwdriver has no labor cost beyond your own time. Contractor-installed cabinet hardware, when bundled into a larger job, commonly runs $3-8 per pull in labor on top of hardware cost — for 20 pulls, that's $60-160 in labor alone for a job that takes most people under two hours to do themselves with matching hole spacing.

**Is stainless steel under matte black powder coat actually more durable than painted aluminum pulls?**

Reviewers consistently report that aluminum pulls with a painted (not powder-coated) black finish chip at the screw-mount points and high-touch edges within 6-12 months, while stainless-steel-core, powder-coated pulls like the Ravinte set are reported holding finish longer under the same daily contact. Powder coating is a baked-on finish, which bonds more durably than sprayed paint.
