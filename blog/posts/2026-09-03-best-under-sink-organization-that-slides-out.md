## How to Measure Your Cabinet Before You Order Either One

Both organizers are returnable, but measuring first avoids the wait. Three numbers matter:

1. **Width between the door hinges** — measure the narrowest point, not the widest, since face-frame cabinets are often narrower at the opening than at the back.
2. **Depth from the door to the back wall** — under-sink cabinets are frequently shallower than kitchen base cabinets elsewhere; 18-20 inches is common under a bathroom vanity versus 22-24 inches under a kitchen sink.
3. **Clearance height under the P-trap** — this is the number that eliminates the most options. If the trap sits low, a two-tier basket organizer may not clear it without the top tier hitting the pipe.

For a fixed-width unit like the Simple Houseware basket, width and depth need to fit within a fairly tight margin since there's no adjustment. For the PXRACK, the expandable frame gives you a range rather than a single number, which is the entire reason it costs more — it converts a measurement problem into a range problem.

Write the three numbers down before opening a listing page. Under-sink returns are disproportionately driven by buyers who measured the door opening but not the depth to the back wall, then discovered the organizer sticks out past the door swing.

## Common Mistakes When Buying Slide-Out Under-Sink Storage

A few patterns show up repeatedly in how these purchases go wrong:

- **Buying fixed-width when the plumbing is off-center.** A centered, symmetric organizer assumes centered, symmetric plumbing. Older vanities and kitchens with a disposal or filter housing often aren't. This is the exact scenario the PXRACK's expandable frame is built to solve.
- **Ignoring trap height.** Width and depth get measured; the vertical clearance under the P-trap gets skipped. That's the number that determines whether a two-tier basket unit will actually sit flat.
- **Assuming "under sink" means one standard size.** Kitchen sink cabinets and bathroom vanity cabinets are not the same depth as a rule — bathroom cabinets run shallower on average, which matters more for a fixed-width organizer than an adjustable one.
- **Not checking door swing clearance.** A slide-out organizer that's deep enough to clear the pipes can still be too deep to let the cabinet doors close, especially on vanities with inset (non-overlay) doors.

The expandable design solves the first mistake structurally rather than requiring precise measurement in advance — you get a range instead of a single tolerance. That's the main argument for paying the extra $12.02 for the PXRACK over the fixed-width basket organizer if there's any doubt about the cabinet's plumbing layout.
