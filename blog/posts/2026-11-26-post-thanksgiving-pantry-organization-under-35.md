## What a Pantry Actually Looks Like the Saturday After Thanksgiving

Pantry-organization searches spike in the days after Thanksgiving for a predictable reason: the holiday generates more loose food than any other week of the year. Half an onion, a quarter stick of butter, two cups of mashed potatoes, a ramekin of cranberry sauce — none of it fits the containers already in the cabinet, so it goes into whatever's on hand. Takeout tubs. Foil-covered bowls. A gallon freezer bag folded over and clipped.

The fix reviewers describe most often isn't more space — it's uniform, stackable, airtight containers sized for actual leftovers rather than bulk dry goods. The Rubbermaid Brilliance 16-Cup Airtight Food Storage Container (clear base, grey lid) holds a 4.7-star average across 31,715 ratings, which is a large enough sample that the complaints are worth reading: a handful of reviewers mention the lid seal loosening after heavy dishwasher cycles, but the majority report the clear Tritan body staying crack-free well past a year of stacking in the fridge. At $19.99, one container that size costs close to what a 2-pack of takeout-style tubs runs at the grocery store, minus the lids that warp in the microwave.

For the week after Thanksgiving specifically, the 16-cup size is the one reviewers point to for turkey and stuffing portions — big enough for a quart-plus of leftovers, small enough to stack three-high on a fridge shelf that already has a pie in it.
