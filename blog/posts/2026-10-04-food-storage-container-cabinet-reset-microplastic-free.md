## FAQ

**Are BPA-free plastic containers actually microplastic-free?**

No. BPA-free means the plastic doesn't contain bisphenol-A, not that it's free of microplastic shedding — Tritan and polypropylene containers can still release particles under heat or scratching. Only glass and stainless steel eliminate plastic-to-food contact entirely.

**How many containers does it take to replace a typical kitchen cabinet?**

Based on the set sizes reviewed here, 20-24 pieces (like Vtopmart's 28,028-rated set) is enough to clear a standard upper cabinet shelf in one purchase, versus buying 4-6 piece sets repeatedly over time.

**What's the price difference between a full plastic swap and a full glass swap?**

The BPA-free plastic sets reviewed here run $17.99 to $29.98 for multi-packs of 5-24 pieces. Glass sets of comparable piece count typically cost more per unit due to weight and shipping, though current pricing should be checked on the listing before buying.

**Do 10-inch Lazy Susan turntables actually fit standard cabinet shelves?**

The 10-inch turntables reviewed here are sized for standard 12-inch-deep cabinet shelves and fridge door shelves. Measure shelf depth before buying, since larger turntables can overhang the shelf edge.

**Will a drain-tray fridge container really make produce last longer?**

Reviewers on the 5,070-rated fruit storage set consistently report produce lasting several days longer than in a sealed container without drainage, since the separate tray keeps condensation away from the food instead of pooling under it.

**Is it worth replacing containers that still have intact lids?**

Only if the current set is already mismatched across more than 2-3 different shapes. The main value of a uniform set is stacking efficiency, which disappears as soon as old, differently-shaped containers get mixed back in, even if those containers are otherwise fine.
