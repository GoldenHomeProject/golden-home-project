## Pre-Treat Dried Spots Before Any Machine Touches Them

Cordless vacuum-mops are built for one continuous pass — they're not designed to scrub at dried-on food or grease spots, and running the roller back and forth over the same stubborn spot repeatedly is what drains the battery fastest and triggers the self-clean cycle sooner. The Scrub Daddy Sponge, priced at $15.15, is built with FlexTexture material that firms up in cold water for scrubbing and softens in warm water for general wiping.

For dried-on spots — syrup, grease splatter, pet accidents — a quick scrub with cold water loosens the residue before the mop or vacuum-mop ever passes over it. That's a cheaper fix than running a $299 machine's roller over the same spot three times, and it protects the roller brush from grinding dried debris into the bristles, which is a common cause of premature roller wear reported on cordless models.

It's dye-free and scratch-free, which matters on finished hardwood and polished tile where an abrasive sponge can leave fine scratches that show up under raking light — exactly the surfaces people buy a $299 vacuum mop to protect in the first place.
