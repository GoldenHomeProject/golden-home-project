## What "Builder-Grade" Actually Means on Your Cabinets

Most tract-home and spec-build kitchens from roughly 2005 to 2018 shipped with one of two hardware defaults: polished brass knobs or plain brushed nickel bar pulls, usually the cheapest SKU the builder could buy in bulk. Neither is broken — they're just generic, and generic is exactly what reads as "not updated" to anyone touring a home for sale or hosting in it.

The fix is mechanical, not aesthetic. Cabinet hardware mounts through pre-drilled holes at a standard center-to-center spacing, most commonly 3 inches (76mm), 3.75 inches (96mm), or 5 inches (128mm) for drawer pulls, and a single hole for knobs. If your existing spacing matches a new pull, the swap is a screwdriver job. If it doesn't, you're either drilling new holes (fine on wood cabinets, patchable with wood filler) or buying pulls sized to your current holes. Measure this before ordering anything — it's the single detail that determines whether this is a 90-minute project or a return-and-reorder cycle.

One more measurement worth taking: count your doors and drawers. A standard 10x10 kitchen typically runs 20 to 30 pulls once you count uppers, lowers, and drawer banks. That's the number that determines whether a 30-pack covers the whole kitchen or leaves you short by the island.

## The Mixed-Metal Trend Publishers Are Pinning This Week

Mixing matte black with brushed brass, warm gold, or nickel on the same wall of cabinets is the specific styling angle showing up on HGTV's Pinterest boards heading into the fall hosting season. The logic designers give for it: an all-black kitchen can read stark or trendy-in-a-dating-itself way, while mixing in one warm metal (usually through the faucet, pendant lighting, or a subset of the hardware) mimics the layered-finish look that custom kitchen designers charge for.

The practical version of this on a budget doesn't require buying two hardware lines. It means putting a uniform matte black pull on the majority of doors and drawers, then letting whatever brass or gold is already in the room — a light fixture, a faucet, cabinet legs — do the warm-metal half of the mix. That's a one-purchase upgrade instead of two.
