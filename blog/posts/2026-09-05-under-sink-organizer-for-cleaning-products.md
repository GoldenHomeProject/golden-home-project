## Measure Before You Buy — and Wipe the Cabinet Out First

Both organizers above are returned most often for the same reason: someone measured the cabinet door opening instead of the usable floor space behind the plumbing. Before ordering either one, measure three numbers: the full width of the cabinet floor, the width of the P-trap and its clearance on each side (if it's centered), and the vertical distance from the cabinet floor to the underside of the sink basin.

If the pipe is centered and each side measures at least a few inches, the PXRACK's expandable drawers can be set to match. If the pipe runs to one side, the Simple Houseware's fixed-width baskets just need the one open rectangle to fit inside.

Worth doing at the same time: pull everything out and wipe the cabinet floor down before the new organizer goes in, since old spills and dust under existing bottles won't disappear once there's a shelf on top of them. ## FAQ

**What size under sink organizer fits a standard kitchen cabinet?**

Measure the width on each side of the P-trap separately, not the full cabinet opening — an expandable organizer like the PXRACK ($46.99) is built to be sized per side when the pipe runs down the center, while a fixed-width unit like the Simple Houseware ($34.97) needs one open rectangle to fit inside.

**Sliding baskets or pull-out drawers — which is easier to use daily?**

Open wire baskets (Simple Houseware) let you see and grab a bottle without pulling the whole tray out; enclosed pull-out drawers (PXRACK, 4.7 stars/1,773 ratings) hide clutter better but add roughly an inch or two more vertical clearance requirement since the frame sits taller.

**Will an under sink organizer work around a garbage disposal?**

Check vertical clearance first — a garbage disposal mount often sits lower than a P-trap and can block a tall drawer-style organizer. Measure from the cabinet floor to the lowest hanging fixture before ordering either the PXRACK or a basket-style unit.

**How do I stop the cabinet from smelling musty after adding an organizer?**

Keep wet sponges off the organizer shelf entirely — a sink-mounted holder like the Cisily Sponge Holder (4.6 stars, 14,151 ratings, $13.99) prevents the damp-sponge-on-a-shelf problem that causes most under-sink mildew, since the sponge dries at the sink instead of trapped against wire or plastic.

**What should go on the top shelf versus the bottom of the organizer?**

Put weekly items — all-purpose spray, a scrub paste like Stardrops The Pink Stuff ($5.97), dish soap refills — on the front-facing top shelf. Push rarely used items like drain opener or oven cleaner to the back or bottom tier.

The shelf itself is the easy part to fix once you know which of the two layouts you're dealing with: a center pipe that needs an expandable, split-drawer organizer like the PXRACK, or a side pipe that leaves one open rectangle for a fixed-width unit like the Simple Houseware baskets. Neither purchase solves anything if the cabinet goes back to a damp sponge and a leaking spray bottle within a month, which is why the sponge holder and the wipe-down step above aren't optional add-ons — they're the difference between an organizer that stays useful and one that's moldy again by winter.
