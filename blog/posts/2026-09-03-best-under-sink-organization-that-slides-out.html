How to Measure Your Cabinet Before You Order Either One

Both organizers are returnable, but measuring first avoids the wait. Three numbers matter:

1. **Width between the door hinges** — measure the narrowest point, not the widest, since face-frame cabinets are often narrower at the opening than at the back. 2. **Depth from the door to the back wall** — under-sink cabinets are frequently shallower than kitchen base cabinets elsewhere; 18-20 inches is common under a bathroom vanity versus 22-24 inches under a kitchen sink. 3. **Clearance height under the P-trap** — this is the number that eliminates the most options. If the trap sits low, a two-tier basket organizer may not clear it without the top tier hitting the pipe.

For a fixed-width unit like the Simple Houseware basket, width and depth need to fit within a fairly tight margin since there's no adjustment. For the PXRACK, the expandable frame gives you a range rather than a single number, which is the entire reason it costs more — it converts a measurement problem into a range problem.

Write the three numbers down before opening a listing page. Under-sink returns are disproportionately driven by buyers who measured the door opening but not the depth to the back wall, then discovered the organizer sticks out past the door swing.

- **Buying fixed-width when the plumbing is off-center.** A centered, symmetric organizer assumes centered, symmetric plumbing. Older vanities and kitchens with a disposal or filter housing often aren't.
