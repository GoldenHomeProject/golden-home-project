## Measure This Before You Order Either One

A standard base cabinet under a bathroom sink runs about 24 inches wide with the doors on, but the usable floor space is almost never the full width. The P-trap and shutoff valves typically sit in the center 6 to 10 inches of that space and start a few inches back from the front edge — that center section is what an expandable organizer's frame has to straddle, not the cabinet opening itself.

Before ordering either organizer, check three measurements:

- **Width on each side of the P-trap**, floor to floor, on the left and right of the pipe — this is the number an expandable frame actually needs to span.
- **Height from the cabinet floor to the underside of the P-trap curve** — a top shelf or basket has to clear this point without the pipe blocking it from sliding fully back.
- **Depth to the back wall or wherever the supply lines exit the cabinet** — a frame that's deep enough front-to-back but hits a valve at the rear won't seat flush.

Cabinets with a garbage disposal, a water filter housing, or an under-sink water softener connection eat more floor space than a standard P-trap alone, and are the most common reason an otherwise well-reviewed under-sink organizer gets a one-star 'didn't fit my cabinet' review. None of that shows up in a product photo — it only shows up with a tape measure in your own cabinet.

## Cool-Down Time and Cord Management Matter More Than the Bin You Buy

Neither organizer solves the two complaints that show up most often in reviews of under-sink storage built for hair tools: heat and cords. Those are routine problems, not product defects, and they follow you into whichever bin you buy.

Most flat irons and curling wands are rated by their manufacturers to stay hot for roughly 15 to 30 minutes after being unplugged, depending on the barrel material and how high the heat setting was. Putting one straight into an enclosed drawer skips that cool-down window entirely — the drawer traps the heat instead of letting it dissipate, which is how plastic organizer interiors end up warped or a nearby brush ends up with a melted flat spot. An open wire basket is somewhat more forgiving because air moves through it, but 'more forgiving' still isn't the same as safe within the first few minutes off the counter.

Cords are the other recurring issue. A flat iron, a curling wand, and a blow dryer between them can easily add three feet of cord that has nowhere obvious to go in either a drawer or a basket, and a tangled cord is the single most common reason people stop using an under-sink system within a few weeks and go back to the counter. A cheap set of silicone cord clips or velcro ties, applied to each tool before it goes in, does more for how long this system actually gets used than which specific organizer you buy.

## FAQ

**What's the actual difference between the PXRACK and Simple Houseware under-sink organizers?**

Pick drawers if you want tools hidden and cords contained; pick baskets if you want airflow and a lower price.

**Will an expandable under-sink organizer fit around my P-trap pipe?**

Expandable frames are designed to adjust around the P-trap and supply lines rather than requiring you to remove them. Measure the usable floor width on either side of the pipe before ordering — the pipe, not the cabinet door opening, is usually the real constraint on fit.

**Is it safe to put a hot flat iron straight into a drawer or basket?**

No. Most flat irons and curling wands stay hot for roughly 15 to 30 minutes after being unplugged. A closed plastic drawer holds that heat in rather than letting it dissipate, and even an open wire basket can transfer enough heat to warp nearby plastic items if the tool goes in within a few minutes of unplugging it.

**Do these organizers require drilling or mounting?**

No. Both the PXRACK and the Simple Houseware are freestanding — they slide into the cabinet on their own frame or rails rather than attaching to the cabinet walls, which matters if you're renting and can't drill.

**How much can a two-drawer or two-tier under-sink organizer actually hold?**

Enough for the core hair-tool set most people store here — a flat iron, a curling wand or two, a diffuser attachment, and a brush or comb — split across the drawers or baskets, with the PXRACK's sliding top shelf typically used for taller bottles.
