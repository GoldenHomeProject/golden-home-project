## Brushed Gold or Matte Black: Which One Actually Sells the 'Renovated' Look

Brushed gold gets more Pinterest saves this quarter — HGTV's beige-and-brown kitchen boards and BHG's cabinet-decor roundups are both leaning warm-metal right now. But gold hardware has a real risk that matte black doesn't: brushed gold finishes are more prone to visible wear at contact points (the underside of a pull, where fingers actually grip) because the gold layer is a thin physical vapor deposition coating over steel or zinc, while matte black stainless is typically a single-tone finish straight through.

On Amazon's verified best-seller data, the matte black stainless option is the one with a large, mature review base (38,159 ratings) confirming long-term wear performance. That's not a knock on gold — it's a data availability issue. If your cabinet boxes are beige, honey oak, or a warm wood tone — the exact 'dated' look this trend targets — matte black creates the highest contrast against the cabinet color, which is what actually reads as 'renovated' in before/after photos. High contrast between hardware and cabinet face is doing more visual work than the specific metal tone. Pair matte black pulls with a brushed-gold or brass faucet if you want the warm accent, and let the hardware carry the contrast instead.

## The 20-Minute Install: What the Hole Spacing Actually Determines

Cabinet pull installation time is almost entirely a function of one variable: whether the new pull's screw holes match the old one's spacing. When they match, it's a screwdriver job — remove the old knob, thread the new pull's screws through the existing holes, done. Amazon's cabinet hardware buying guides and the Ravinte listing both specify 3-inch (76mm) and 5-inch (128mm) center-to-center spacing, which covers the two most common builder-grade standards in U.S. kitchens built after roughly 1995.

If the spacing doesn't match — say, swapping a single-hole round knob for a two-hole pull — the job requires filling the old hole with wood filler, sanding flush, and drilling a new hole at the correct spacing, which adds real time (measure twice, use painter's tape as a drill guide to avoid splintering the cabinet face) and is the step most likely to go wrong on a rental unit you need to restore later.

The fastest path, and the one that keeps a rental deposit intact, is matching hole spacing to begin with: measure the existing knob's screw spacing with a tape measure before ordering, then buy a pull rated for that exact spacing rather than assuming. A cordless drill with a Phillips bit speeds up a full 30-door kitchen from roughly ninety minutes by hand to about twenty, which is where the transformation's headline number comes from — twenty pulls at roughly one minute each once the first few establish a rhythm.

## FAQ

**Will new cabinet pulls fit the holes my existing knobs left behind?**

Only if the hole spacing matches. Measure the screw-to-screw distance on an existing knob before buying — most U.S. builder-grade cabinets from after 1995 use either 3-inch (76mm) single-hole spacing or 5-inch (128mm) two-hole spacing, which is what the Ravinte 30-pack ($26.99) is listed for. If your spacing doesn't match either standard, the job requires filling and redrilling.

**Do I need a landlord's permission to swap cabinet hardware in a rental?**

Most leases allow hardware swaps as long as the original knobs are kept and reinstalled before move-out, since no new holes are drilled when the spacing matches. Check your lease's alterations clause specifically; if drilling new holes is required because the spacing doesn't match, that's a bigger ask and worth confirming in writing first.

**How many cabinet doors and drawers does a 30-pack actually cover?**

30 pulls covers roughly a full standard kitchen — most U.S. kitchens run 20 to 30 cabinet doors and drawers combined, based on typical builder layouts. A larger kitchen or one with double-pull drawers may need a second pack.

**What tools do I need for the install?**

A Phillips screwdriver is enough if hole spacing matches and the old knobs come off cleanly. A cordless drill speeds the job up (roughly 20 minutes for a full kitchen vs. 60–90 minutes by hand) but isn't required. Painter's tape as a drill guide is useful only if new holes need to be drilled.

**Is matte black or brushed gold the better resale choice?**

Matte black stainless has the larger verified review base on Amazon (38,159 ratings at 4.7 stars for the Ravinte pulls) confirming long-term wear resistance, since the finish runs through the metal rather than sitting as a thin coating. Brushed gold looks warmer in current design content but wears at grip points faster because gold finishes are typically a thin plated layer.

**Will matte black stainless pulls rust or discolor near a sink?**

Stainless steel construction, which the Ravinte pulls use, resists rust and water spotting significantly better than plated brass or zinc alloy hardware, which is the more common complaint in lower-rated cabinet hardware listings near sink and dishwasher areas.
