Why Hardware Moves Resale Perception Faster Than Paint Does

Builder-grade cabinets usually fail in one of two ways: dated brass hardware from a 1990s-2000s build, or plain white boxes with the scuffed plastic knobs that came standard on entry-level cabinet lines. Neither is a structural problem — the doors and boxes are often fine — but both signal 'not updated' to a buyer or renter in under three seconds, which is about how long real estate photos get before someone scrolls past.

BHG's current resale-value cabinet piece and Apartment Therapy's cabinet-painting tutorial are both circulating this week, and both treat the fix as a paint project: strip, sand, prime, spray, cure, rehang. That's a real weekend of labor and a real risk of brush marks or tacky paint if the timeline gets compressed. Hardware is the part of that same visual update you can do without touching the doors at all. The finish and shape of a knob or pull does a disproportionate amount of the 'does this look current' work — it's the part of the cabinet your hand actually touches and your eye lands on at counter height.

The tradeoff is honesty-worthy: hardware alone doesn't fix a door with chipped laminate, water damage, or a finish that's genuinely gone. If the box and door material are in bad shape, paint or refacing is still the right call. But for cabinets that are structurally fine and just read as outdated because of brass or white-plastic hardware, the hardware is the cheaper, faster, and more reversible lever to pull first.

The Measurement That Decides Whether a 30-Pack Fits Your Doors

Before ordering any hardware kit, the number that matters is center-to-center hole spacing — the distance between the two screw holes already drilled in your cabinet doors and drawers, not the overall length of the pull. Standard spacing options on the market run 3 inches (76mm), 3.75 inches (96mm), 5 inches (128mm), and 6.3 inches (160mm). A 5-inch pull typically ships with a hole spacing in the 3-inch to 3.75-inch range depending on the exact listing, so measure your existing holes with a tape measure before buying rather than assuming length equals spacing.

If your current hardware uses a different spacing, you have two options: drill new holes (using a template, since off-by-a-millimeter holes are visible) or accept you'll be living with two sets of holes if you ever revert. Single-hole knobs sidestep this problem entirely since they only need one existing hole to line up, which is part of why knobs remain the safer choice on doors with mismatched hole patterns.

On quantity: a kitchen with 10 upper doors, 8 lower doors, and 6 drawers needs roughly 24 pieces of hardware. A 30-pack covers that layout with 6 pieces left over for a pantry door, an island, or a bathroom vanity that could use the same finish. Reviewers on the larger 30-pack listings consistently report buying a second pack only when a kitchen runs past 30 total doors and drawers — which is common in kitchens with a large island or a butler's pantry.

Matte Black vs. Brushed Gold: Which Finish Actually Reads 'Resale-Ready'

The current resale-value cabinet coverage from BHG leans toward mixed-metal kitchens — brushed gold or brass fixtures on faucets and lighting paired with matte black cabinet hardware — rather than an all-one-finish look. The logic is practical: faucets and lighting are expensive to replace, so matching new cabinet hardware to those existing fixtures (instead of the other way around) avoids a second renovation.

Matte black has become the volume leader in cabinet pulls specifically because it reads as neutral against almost any counter or wall color — white Shaker, gray Shaker, navy island, it doesn't clash. It also hides fingerprints and water spots better than polished brass or chrome, which matters on pulls that get touched dozens of times a day. Reviewers across the major matte black pull listings repeatedly mention this as a reason for repeat purchases across multiple rooms, not just the kitchen.

Brushed gold reads warmer and pairs better with warm-toned wood cabinets or brass lighting already in place. The honest tradeoff: brushed gold finishes show fingerprints and water spots more visibly than matte black over time, based on the pattern of complaints on brushed-finish listings generally, so it asks for more frequent wiping near the sink and stove. If your existing lighting and faucet are chrome or black, matte black cabinet hardware is the lower-risk match. If they're brass or gold-toned, matching the hardware to them avoids an oddly mismatched kitchen more than it costs you in maintenance.

Frequently asked

Will a 5-inch cabinet pull fit the holes my old knobs left behind?

Only if the spacing matches. A single-hole knob can go anywhere, but a two-hole pull needs its center-to-center spacing (commonly 3 inches or 3.75 inches for a 5-inch pull) to match your existing holes exactly. Measure before ordering — mismatched spacing means drilling new holes or returning the pack.

Do knobs or pulls add more resale value?

Current resale-focused coverage from outlets like BHG favors pulls on drawers and larger doors because they read as more current, with knobs reserved for smaller cabinet doors. A mixed kit — pulls on drawers, knobs on doors — is the combination most often recommended, rather than one style used everywhere.

Is a 30-pack enough hardware for an average kitchen?

Usually yes. A kitchen with 10 upper doors, 8 lower doors, and 6 drawers needs about 24 pieces, leaving 6 spares from a 30-pack. Kitchens with a large island or butler's pantry that push past 30 total doors and drawers will need a second pack.

Can I mix matte black cabinet hardware with brushed gold faucets and lighting?

Yes — mixed-metal kitchens pairing matte black cabinet pulls with brushed gold or brass fixtures are a current trend specifically because replacing cabinet hardware is far cheaper than replacing a faucet or light fixture to match.

How long does it take to install 30 cabinet pulls?

With a cordless drill and existing holes that already match the new hole spacing, each pull takes about 2-3 minutes to install with two screws. A full kitchen of 24-30 pulls runs roughly one to two hours, based on the time reviewers commonly report for a full-kitchen swap.

What's the most common complaint on large matte black pull packs?

Slight finish inconsistency between individual pulls within the same pack, reported by a small percentage of buyers, and buyers who ordered without measuring hole spacing first. Checking recent reviews for batch-consistency mentions before ordering reduces the risk of either.
