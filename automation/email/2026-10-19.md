# The $89 kit behind this week's cabinet trend

**Preheader:** Plus what it really costs once you factor in a second kit and the cure time.

---

Hi friend,

Every contractor I called about these cabinets started the quote the same way: tear them out, order new boxes, budget $5,000 to $20,000, and plan on three to six weeks without a working kitchen.

I almost believed that was the only option. Then I noticed HGTV, Better Homes & Gardens, and Apartment Therapy all running the same story this week — warm, burnt-orange and terracotta cabinet tones, and a push toward painting instead of replacing. So I went looking at what that actually takes to pull off at home, starting with an $89 cabinet paint kit people keep mentioning.

Here's the part nobody leads with: that kit doesn't come in burnt-orange. It ships in a handful of fixed colors — Dark, Linen, Espresso, White — and the terracotta tones all over Pinterest right now come from taking the kit's bonding coat to a paint counter and having it custom-tinted. That's also why two people using the same $89 kit end up with noticeably different results — tint ratio, how the oak grain takes the color, and how many coats go on all shift the final look.

Then there's the real cost. The label says 200 square feet of coverage per kit, which is about 20 to 25 doors and drawers. Most kitchens with a double run of upper and lower cabinets need two kits, not one — so the honest number is closer to $160-$180, not $89. And cure time runs longer than I expected: dry to the touch in about 2 hours, but full cure stretching 1 to 3 weeks, with doors off the hinges that whole time.

That window off the hinges turns out to be useful, though. It's the cheapest moment to swap in matte black hardware — a 30-pack runs $26.99 and the screw spacing lines up with most existing pilot holes, so there's no redrilling. It's also the one point where fixing a cluttered corner cabinet costs zero extra disruption, since everything's already pulled out onto the counter anyway.

I put the full breakdown together — what's actually in the box, what reviewers flag about ventilation and coat count, the real total cost once you account for a second kit, and the hardware and organizing swaps worth making while the doors are off. If you've got oak cabinets that are structurally fine but dated, it's worth five minutes before you call a contractor.
